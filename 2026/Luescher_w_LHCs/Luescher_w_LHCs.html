Derivation of Lüscher’s formula

\delta-function interaction

The contact interaction is regulated by a hard cutoff \Lambda,

\langle \vec p\,' | \hat V_\delta | \vec p \rangle = \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m}\,\theta(\Lambda - p')\,\theta(\Lambda - p) \, ,

where a(\Lambda) is tuned to reproduce the T-matrix at threshold,

a(\Lambda) = \frac{a_0}{1 - \tfrac{2}{\pi} a_0 \Lambda} \, ,

and a_0 is the physical s-wave scattering length.

Scattering amplitude in free space

Because this interaction is separable, the bubble sum can be summed geometrically to give

\begin{aligned} T(\vec p, -\vec p; E) &= \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)/m}{1 - \tfrac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m}\int\! \tfrac{d^3 q}{(2\pi)^3}\, \tfrac{1}{E - q^2/m + i\varepsilon}} \\[4pt] &= \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)/m}{1 - \tfrac{2 a(\Lambda)}{\pi}\int\! dq\, \tfrac{q^2}{mE - q^2 + i\varepsilon}} \\[4pt] &= \frac{4\pi/m}{\tfrac{1}{a(\Lambda)} - \tfrac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\Lambda\! dq \left(\mathcal{P}\tfrac{q^2}{mE - q^2} - i\pi q^2 \delta(mE - q^2)\right)} \end{aligned}

The bubble sum using the contact interaction.

Evaluating the denominator

\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{a(\Lambda)} &- \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\Lambda\! dq \left(\mathcal{P}\tfrac{q^2}{mE - q^2} - i\pi q^2 \delta(mE - q^2)\right) \\[4pt] &= \frac{1}{a(\Lambda)} + \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\Lambda - \tfrac{\sqrt{mE}}{2}\log\Big(\tfrac{\Lambda + \sqrt{mE}}{\Lambda - \sqrt{mE}}\Big) + i\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{mE}\right) \\[4pt] &= \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{\sqrt{mE}}{\pi}\log\Big(\tfrac{\Lambda + \sqrt{mE}}{\Lambda - \sqrt{mE}}\Big) + i\sqrt{mE} \, . \end{aligned}

Resulting amplitude

Plugging this back in gives

\begin{aligned} T(\vec p, -\vec p; E) &= \frac{4\pi/m}{\tfrac{1}{a_0} - \tfrac{\sqrt{mE}}{\pi}\log\Big(\tfrac{\Lambda + \sqrt{mE}}{\Lambda - \sqrt{mE}}\Big) + i\sqrt{mE}} \\[4pt] &\approx \frac{4\pi/m}{\tfrac{1}{a_0} + i\sqrt{mE} - \tfrac{2mE}{\pi}\tfrac{1}{\Lambda} + O(\Lambda^{-2})} \, . \end{aligned}

  • At threshold one recovers the correct normalization, T(\vec 0, \vec 0; E=0) = 4\pi a_0/m.

  • Assuming the existence of a bound state E=-B, one gets the familiar result from the pole of the amplitude: B=\frac{1}{ma_0^2}.

Lüscher’s formula

Within a finite cubic volume of size L^3, the analog of the denominator evaluated above is

\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{a(\Lambda)} - \frac{4\pi}{m}\frac{1}{L^3}\sum_{\vec n}^{2\pi n < \Lambda L}\frac{1}{E - \tfrac{4\pi^2 n^2}{m L^2}} &= \frac{1}{a(\Lambda)} - \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\vec n}^{2\pi n < \Lambda L}\frac{1}{\epsilon - n^2} \\[4pt] &= \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{2}{\pi}\Lambda - \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\vec n}^{2\pi n < \Lambda L}\frac{1}{\epsilon - n^2} \, , \end{aligned}

where I define \epsilon = \dfrac{E m L^2}{4\pi^2}.

Lüscher’s formula (cont.)

\Lambda above has dimension of inverse length. Moving to a dimensionless definition of \Lambda,

\Lambda \to \frac{2\pi}{L} \Lambda\, ,

we now have1

\frac{1}{a_0} - 4\frac{\Lambda}{L} - \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{\epsilon - n^2} = \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{1}{\pi L}\left(\sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{\epsilon - n^2} + 4\pi\Lambda\right) \, ,

which we can rewrite as

-\frac{1}{a_0}=\frac{1}{\pi L}\lim_{\Lambda \to \infty}\left(\sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon} - 4\pi\Lambda\right)\equiv \frac{1}{\pi L}S_0\left(\epsilon\right) \tag{1}

and the RHS is now Lüscher’s formula. In particular, values of \epsilon that satisfy this equality correspond to the eigen-energies of the two-particle interacting system.

  1. The limit \Lambda \to \infty is tacitly assumed, but sometimes I write it explicitly.

Interpretation: -\frac{L}{a_0}=\frac{1}{\pi}S_0\left(\epsilon\right)

  • Given horizontal line -L/a_0, the allowed quantized energies \epsilon occur at the intersection of line with the dashed curves

The full expression

To obtain the full model-independent Lüscher formula, the idea is to generalize the potential

\langle \vec p\,' | \hat V | \vec p \rangle = \theta(\Lambda - p')\left(C_0(\Lambda)+C_2(\Lambda)\frac{p'^2+p^2}{2}+\ldots\right)\theta(\Lambda - p) \, ,

where the coefficients C_i(\Lambda) are tuned such that the scattering amplitude gives the correct physical phase shift \delta,

T(\vec p,-\vec p;E) = -\frac{4\pi}{m}\frac{1}{p\cot\delta - ip} \tag{2}

When we repeat the calculation in a finite volume with this potential, it leads to the full Lüscher formula

p\cot\delta = \frac{1}{\pi L}S_0(\epsilon)\ . where \epsilon is defined as before.

Lüscher’s formula in action

Toy Model

I assume a Hamiltonian with potential of the form

\langle \vec p' | \hat V | \vec p \rangle = V(p',p) = \frac{2\pi C_0}{\mu} f(p') f(p) f(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{\pi (p r)^2}{32}\right) C_0^{-1} = \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{4}{\pi r} \, ,

This is a separable potential with parameters chosen such that

p \cot\delta(p) = \frac{1}{f(p)^2}\left(-\frac{1}{a_0} + \frac{2p}{\sqrt\pi} F\!\left(\tfrac{1}{4} p \sqrt\pi r\right)\right) \approx -\frac{1}{a_0} + \frac{r}{2} p^2 - \frac{r^3}{48} p^4 + \mathcal{O}(p^6)

Procedure when particles are put in a box

In a box of side length L, the eigenenergies E can be obtained by solving the following transcendental equation

-\frac{L}{a_0} + \frac{4L}{\pi r} = \frac{1}{\pi} \sum_{\vec n \in \text{B.Z.}} \frac{f(\vec n)^2}{\vec n^2 - \epsilon} \, ,

with \epsilon = \dfrac{E mL^2}{4\pi^2} and f(\vec n) = \exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi^3 r^2}{8 L^2} \vec n^2\right). I assume the two particles have equal mass m.

Given \epsilon, I insert this into Lüscher’s formula to get the phase shift

p\cot\delta(p) = \frac{1}{\pi L} S_0(\epsilon)

where

S_0(\epsilon) = \lim_{\Lambda \to \infty} \left[ \sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda} \frac{1}{n^2 - \epsilon} - 4\pi\Lambda \right]

When does Lüscher’s formula fail?

  • Lüscher’s formula is derived assuming only short-ranged interactions (\ll L)
    • This results in power-law corrections (1/L) to on-shell scattering
    • Exponential corrections to bound states
  • t-channel exchange via one-pion exchange (long range, near on-shell) introduces a left-hand cut (lhc)
    • DD^* scattering example: \big(p^{1\pi}_{\rm lhc}\big)^2 = -\frac{\mu^2}{4}, \qquad \mu^2 = m_\pi^2 - \Delta M^2, \qquad \Delta M = M_{D^*}-M_D
  • When m_\pi^2\sim \Delta M^2 then lhc is near threshold and ERE breakes down\implies Lüscher’s formula no longer applicable

Current strategies to deal with left-hand cuts

  • Keep the OPE explicit and apply Lüscher only to the short-range remainder
    • Lüscher equation with long-range forces 2402.12985
  • Go full three-body, treating the pion as dynamical since it goes on-shell
    • Incorporating DD\pi effects and left-hand cuts in lattice QCD studies of the T_{cc}(3875)^+ 2401.06609
  • Extend the formalism onto the cut itself
    • Finite-volume scattering on the left-hand cut 2311.18793
  • Solve a finite-volume Schrödinger equation in a plane-wave basis with chiral EFT potentials, using half-off-shell potentials that stay analytic below threshold and dodging partial-wave truncation
    • Left-hand cut problem in lattice QCD and an EFT-based solution 2503.23944

Can we use separable potentials to include left-hand cuts?

  • I will only consider s-wave scattering to keep life as simple as possible.

  • The “long-range” part is modeled by one-pion exchange (OPE)1

V^{l=0}_{\pi}(p',p) = -\frac{g^2}{4p'p}\log\left(\frac{(p+p')^2+\mu^2}{(p-p')^2+\mu^2}\right)

  • The branch cut can be seen by setting p' = p and noting that the log’s argument becomes negative for

p^2 < -\mu^2/4

  • Assuming there is a way to do this, I want to expand this long-range potential in a separable basis, i.e.

V^{l=0}_{\pi}(p',p) = \sum_{i,j}^{\infty} g_i(p') \lambda_{ij} g_j(p)\approx \sum_{i,j}^{N} g_i(p') \lambda_{ij} g_j(p)

  • The question is then: what is a good choice of basis functions g_i(p) (or form factors) that can reproduce the branch cut?

    • Typically, one would use a polynomial basis that is analytic in the complex plane (i.e. “entire”), but this will not reproduce the branch cut.

  1. \mu\sim m_\pi is the pion mass and g is the dimensionless pion coupling constant (g=\frac{g_A\mu}{2f_\pi}).

V_{\rm cut}(p',p): a separable potential that reproduces (most of) the branch cut

  • So here’s an idea: Consider the following expression:

V_{\rm cut}(p',p)\equiv-\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2}\left[h(p')+h(p)\right] where h(p) = \frac{\mu^2}{4p^2} \log\left(\frac{4 p^2+\mu^2}{\mu^2}\right)

Properties of V_{\rm cut}(p',p)

  • V_{\rm cut}(p'=p) has the same on-shell branch cut structure as V^{l=0}_{\pi}(p'=p)
  • symmetric in p and p'
  • it is separable

Warning

  • V_{\rm cut}(p',p) does not reproduce the correct full off-shell singularity structure!!!
  • So the idea is to use the following expression:

V^{l=0}_{\pi}(p',p) = V_{\rm cut}(p',p) + \underbrace{V^{l=0}_{\pi}(p',p)-V_{\rm cut}(p',p)}_{\approx\sum_{i,j=2}^{N} g_i(p') \lambda_{ij} g_j(p)}

  • The functions g_i(p) are analytic in the complex plane

Let’s add this potential to the \delta-term from before

\begin{align} \hat V &= \hat V^{l=0}_\pi + \hat V_\delta \nonumber\\ &= \hat V_{\rm cut} + ( \hat V^{l=0}_\pi - \hat V_{\rm cut} ) + \hat V_\delta \nonumber\\ &= -\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2}\left[\hat h \hat\delta_\Lambda+ \hat\delta_\Lambda\hat h\right] + \sum_{i,j=2}^{N} \hat g_i \hat\delta_\Lambda \lambda_{ij}\hat g_j + \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} \hat\delta_\Lambda\nonumber \end{align}

where \hat\delta_\Lambda is the regulated delta function defined as \langle p'| \hat\delta_\Lambda|p\rangle=\theta(\Lambda - p')\,\theta(\Lambda - p), and \hat h and \hat g_i are operators defined such that \hat h|p\rangle = h(p)|p\rangle and \hat g_i|p\rangle = g_i(p)|p\rangle.

I can rexpress this potential in rank-(N+1) form. I first define the vector of form factors \hat{\bf f} = (1,\hat h,\hat g_2,\ldots,\hat g_N). The potential is then \hat V = \hat{\bf f}^T {\hat\lambda} \hat{\bf f}, where \lambda is

{\hat \lambda} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} & -\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ -\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2} & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda_{22} & \cdots & \lambda_{2N} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda_{N2} & \cdots & \lambda_{NN} \end{pmatrix}\ \hat\delta_\Lambda \, .

Now consider the T-matrix

\hat T(E)=\hat V + \hat V \hat G_0(E) \hat T(E)

Assume

\hat T(E) = \hat{\bf f}^T \tau(E) \hat{\bf f}

and the beauty of separability comes into play:

\tau^{-1}(E) = \begin{cases} \lambda^{-1} - \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int^\Lambda d^3pf(p) G_0 (p;E) f^T(p) & {\rm \infty\ volume}\\ \lambda^{-1} - \frac{1}{L^3}\sum_n^\Lambda f(p_n) G_0 (p_n;E) f^T(p_n) & {\rm finite\ volume} \end{cases}

Test case: N=2

This is equivalent to setting g_i(p)=0\ \forall\ i \implies f(p) = (1,h(p)). We thus have

\lambda = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} & -\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2} \\ -\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2} & 0 \end{pmatrix} \implies \lambda^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} \\ -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} & -\frac{4\mu^4}{g^4}\frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} \end{pmatrix}

Re-tuning a(\Lambda)

We have to retune a(\Lambda) to give the correct scattering amplitude at threshold in the continuum. Note that V_{\rm cut} is also linearly divergent. This complicates things, as we will see.

So the condition I want to match is

T(p'=p=0;E=0)=\frac{4\pi a_0}{m}={\bf f}^T(0)\tau(0){\bf f}(0)=\tau_{11}(0)+\tau_{12}(0)+\tau_{21}(0)+\tau_{22}(0)

We have then that

\begin{align} \tau^{-1}(0)&= \underbrace{ \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} \\ -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} & -\frac{4\mu^4}{g^4}\frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} \end{pmatrix}}_{\lambda^{-1}} - \underbrace{ \int^\Lambda \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3} \begin{pmatrix} \frac{-1}{p^2/m} & -\left(\frac{\mu}{2p}\right)^2\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^2/m}\\ -\left(\frac{\mu}{2p}\right)^2\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^2/m} & -\left(\frac{\mu}{2p}\right)^4\frac{\log^2\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^2/m} \end{pmatrix}}_{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int^\Lambda d^3pf(p) G_0 (p;E=0) f^T(p)}\nonumber\\ &= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} \\ -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} & -\frac{4\mu^4}{g^4}\frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} \end{pmatrix}+ \frac{m}{2\pi^2}\int^\Lambda dp \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^2\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^2}\\ \left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^2\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^2} & \left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^4\frac{\log^2\left(1+\frac{4p^2}{\mu^2}\right)}{p^4} \end{pmatrix}\nonumber\\ &= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} \\ -\frac{2\mu^2}{g^2} & -\frac{4\mu^4}{g^4}\frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m} \end{pmatrix}+ \frac{m}{2\pi^2} \begin{pmatrix} \Lambda & \frac{\pi \mu }{2}-\Delta_1(\Lambda)\\ \frac{\pi \mu }{2}-\Delta_1(\Lambda)& \frac{2 \pi \mu }{3}(1-\log (2))-\Delta_2(\Lambda) \end{pmatrix}\nonumber \end{align}

where

\begin{align} \Delta_1(\Lambda)&=\frac{\mu ^2 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{4 \Lambda }+\mu \cot ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(\Lambda)}{\Lambda}\right)\nonumber\\ \Delta_2(\Lambda)&=\frac{1}{144} \mu \left[96 \left(\log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)+2 \log \left(\frac{2 \mu }{\mu +2 i \Lambda }\right)-2\right) \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)+\frac{3 \mu \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right) \left(\mu ^2 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)+16 \Lambda ^2\right)}{\Lambda ^3}\right.\nonumber\\ &\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+96 i \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)^2+96 i \text{Li}_2\left(\frac{4 i \Lambda }{2 i \Lambda +\mu }-1\right)+8 \pi (12+i \pi -6 \log (4))\right]\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log^2(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^3}\right)\nonumber \end{align}

I can solve for a(\Lambda)1

\begin{align} \left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}a_0\Lambda\right)a(\Lambda)&=a_0 +g^2 m \left(\frac{a_0 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{8 \pi ^2 \Lambda }-\frac{a_0 \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)}{2 \pi ^2 \mu }+\frac{1}{4 \pi \mu ^2}\right)\nonumber\\ & + g^4 m^2 \left[\frac{a_0 \log ^2\left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{192 \pi ^4 \Lambda ^2}+\frac{a_0 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{48 \pi ^4 \mu ^2}+\frac{\frac{\log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{192 \pi ^3 \mu ^2}-\frac{a_0 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right) \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)}{32 \pi ^4 \mu }}{\Lambda }+\frac{a_0 \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)^2}{16 \pi ^4 \mu ^2}+\Lambda \left(\frac{1}{32 \pi ^3 \mu ^4}-\frac{a_0 \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)}{12 \pi ^4 \mu ^3}\right)+\right.\nonumber\\ &\qquad\left.(\pi -2 a_0 \Lambda ) \left(\left(-\frac{\log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{48 \pi ^4 \mu ^3}-\frac{\log \left(\frac{2 \mu }{\mu +2 i \Lambda }\right)}{24 \pi ^4 \mu ^3}\right) \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)-\frac{i \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)^2}{48 \pi ^4 \mu ^3}+\frac{-8 i \pi ^2 \mu -96 i \mu \text{Li}_2\left(\frac{4 i \Lambda }{2 i \Lambda +\mu }-1\right)}{4608 \pi ^4 \mu ^4}\right)-\frac{\log ^2\left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)}{1536 \pi ^3 \Lambda ^3}-\frac{\tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)}{48 \pi ^3 \mu ^3}\right]\nonumber \end{align}

In the limit that g\to0 I find

\frac{a_0}{1-\frac{2}{\pi}a_0\Lambda}+\frac{g^2 m \left(a_0 \mu ^2 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}+1\right)-4 a_0 \Lambda \mu \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 \Lambda }{\mu }\right)+2 \pi \Lambda \right)}{(1-\frac{2}{\pi}a_0\Lambda)8 \pi ^2 \Lambda \mu ^2}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^3\right)

which shows that one recovers the \delta-function result at g=0.

However, in the limit \Lambda\to\infty I get instead the following result:

\begin{align} \frac{1}{1-\frac{2}{\pi}a_0\Lambda}&\left[a_0 +\frac{g^2 m \left(3 a_0 g^2 \mu m-48 \pi a_0 \mu ^2-2 g^2 m \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}\right) +4 g^2 m \log (\Lambda )-4 g^2 m \log (\mu )-2 g^2 m+48 \pi \mu \right)}{192 \pi ^2 \mu ^3}\right.\nonumber\\ &+\left.\Lambda \left(\frac{a_0 g^4 m^2 \log \left(\frac{4 \Lambda ^2}{\mu ^2}\right)}{48 \pi ^3 \mu ^3}-\frac{a_0 g^4 m^2 \log (\Lambda )}{24 \pi ^3 \mu ^3}-\frac{a_0 g^4 m^2}{24 \pi ^3 \mu ^3}+\frac{a_0 g^4 m^2 \log (\mu )}{24 \pi ^3 \mu ^3}+\frac{g^4 m^2}{32 \pi ^3 \mu ^4}\right) +\mathcal{O}\left(\Lambda^{-1}\right)\right]\nonumber \end{align}

The limits g\to0 and \Lambda\to\infty do not commute.

  1. Note that \Lambda is dimensionful.

In a finite volume I have1

\begin{align} \frac{m}{4\pi^2 L}\sum_n^\Lambda f\left(\frac{2\pi |n|}{L}\right) \frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon} f^T\left(\frac{2\pi |n|}{L}\right) =& \frac{m}{4\pi^2 L}\sum_n^\Lambda \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon} & \frac{h\left(2\pi |n|/L\right)}{n^2-\epsilon} \\ \frac{h\left(2\pi |n|/L\right)}{n^2-\epsilon} & \frac{h^2\left(2\pi |n|/L\right)}{n^2-\epsilon} \end{pmatrix}\nonumber\\ =& \frac{m}{4\pi^2 L} \begin{pmatrix} S_0(\epsilon)+4\pi\Lambda-\frac{4\pi\epsilon}{\Lambda} & (\frac{\mu m}{4\pi})^2S_1(\epsilon) \\ (\frac{\mu m}{4\pi})^2S_1(\epsilon) & (\frac{\mu m}{4\pi})^4S_2(\epsilon) \end{pmatrix} \equiv {\color{red}-}\mathcal{G}(\epsilon)\nonumber \end{align}

where I define

S_0(\epsilon)= \lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\left(\sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon} - 4\pi\Lambda\right) \quad;\quad S_1(\epsilon)= \sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon}\frac{1}{n^2}\log\left(1+\left(\frac{4\pi}{\mu L}\right)^2 n^2\right)\quad;\quad S_2(\epsilon)= \sum_{\vec n}^{n < \Lambda}\frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon}\frac{1}{n^4}\log^2\left(1+\left(\frac{4\pi}{\mu L}\right)^2 n^2\right)

Note that the sums in S_1(\epsilon) and S_2(\epsilon) converge and require no counterterm, and that both retain the full 1/(n^2-\epsilon) — nothing is expanded in \epsilon.

I obtain the quantization condition (QC) by demanding

\det(\mathbb{1}_2-\lambda\mathcal{G}(\epsilon))= \det \begin{pmatrix} 1+\frac{4 \Lambda a(\Lambda )}{L}+\frac{a(\Lambda ) S_0(\epsilon )}{\pi L}-\frac{4 \epsilon a(\Lambda )}{\Lambda L}-\frac{g^2 m^3 S_1(\epsilon )}{128 \pi ^4 L} & \frac{\mu ^2 m^2 a(\Lambda ) S_1(\epsilon )}{16 \pi ^3 L}-\frac{g^2 \mu ^2 m^5 S_2(\epsilon )}{2048 \pi ^6 L} \\ -\frac{g^2 \Lambda m}{2 \pi \mu ^2 L}-\frac{g^2 m S_0(\epsilon )}{8 \pi ^2 \mu ^2 L}+\frac{g^2 m \epsilon }{2 \pi \Lambda \mu ^2 L} & 1-\frac{g^2 m^3 S_1(\epsilon )}{128 \pi ^4 L} \end{pmatrix}=0

The QC then gives

1+\frac{4 \Lambda a(\Lambda )}{L}+\frac{a(\Lambda ) S_0(\epsilon )}{\pi L}-\frac{4 \epsilon a(\Lambda )}{\Lambda L}-\frac{g^4 m^6 S_0(\epsilon ) S_2(\epsilon )}{16384 \pi ^8 L^2}+\frac{g^4 m^6 S_1(\epsilon )^2}{16384 \pi ^8 L^2}-\frac{g^4 \Lambda m^6 S_2(\epsilon )}{4096 \pi ^7 L^2}+\frac{g^4 m^6 \epsilon S_2(\epsilon )}{4096 \pi ^7 \Lambda L^2}-\frac{g^2 m^3 S_1(\epsilon )}{64 \pi ^4 L}=0

  1. Note that \Lambda in this case is dimensionless! (i.e. dimensionful \Lambda\to\frac{2\pi}{L}\Lambda)

Why this stays tractable: the determinant collapses

With \lambda_{22}=0 write \lambda=\begin{pmatrix}A & B\\ B& 0\end{pmatrix}, A\equiv\frac{4\pi a(\Lambda)}{m}, B\equiv-\frac{g^2}{2\mu^2}. Then the 2\times2 determinant collapses to

\boxed{\ \det(\mathbb{1}_2-\lambda\mathcal{G}) = 1 - A\,\mathcal{G}_{11} - 2B\,\mathcal{G}_{12} + B^2\left(\mathcal{G}_{12}^2-\mathcal{G}_{11}\mathcal{G}_{22}\right)\ }

Three things this buys

  • Linear in a(\Lambda) — only \mathcal{G}_{11} multiplies A, so the QC solves for a(\Lambda) in one step. No nonlinear system, no \det\lambda in a denominator.
  • The g-counting is manifest: B\propto g^2 and B^2\propto g^4, so the \mathcal{O}(g^0), \mathcal{O}(g^2) and \mathcal{O}(g^4) pieces are the four terms above, in order.
  • No \lambda^{-1} — essential here, since \det\lambda = -B^2 vanishes at g=0 when \lambda_{22}=0. Writing the QC as \det(\lambda^{-1}-\mathcal{G}) would be singular in precisely the limit I use as a check.

Matching term by term against the QC above: -A\mathcal{G}_{11} gives the three a(\Lambda) terms, -2B\mathcal{G}_{12} gives -\frac{g^2m^3S_1}{64\pi^4L}, and B^2(\mathcal{G}_{12}^2-\mathcal{G}_{11}\mathcal{G}_{22}) gives all four g^4 terms ✔️

Is it inconsistent to keep the \mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\Lambda) term only in \mathcal{G}_{11}?

No — recall S_1 and S_2 retain the full 1/(n^2-\epsilon), so the only truncation anywhere is the \Lambda-tail. Sorting those tails by power of \epsilon:

\epsilon^0 tail (fixes a_0) \epsilon^1 tail (fixes r_0)
\mathcal{G}_{11} absorbed into 4\pi\Lambda \mathcal{O}(\epsilon/\Lambda)   kept
\mathcal{G}_{12} \mathcal{O}(\log\Lambda/\Lambda) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon\log\Lambda/\Lambda^3)
\mathcal{G}_{22} \mathcal{O}(\log^2\Lambda/\Lambda^3) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon\log^2\Lambda/\Lambda^5)

In each sector the retained term beats the dropped ones by \Lambda^2. The asymmetry simply reflects that the \mathcal{G}_{11} integrand falls as 1/n^2, whereas S_1 and S_2 fall as \log n/n^4 and \log^2 n/n^6.

I can solve for a_0, and this gives a very complicated expression which I won’t write out here. If I consider the g\to 0 limit (holding \Lambda fixed), I find1

\begin{align} -\frac{1}{a_0}+\frac{4 \epsilon }{\Lambda L}&=\frac{S_0(\epsilon )}{\pi L}+g^2 \left(\frac{\Lambda m^3 S_1(\epsilon )}{16 \pi ^4 L^2}-\frac{m^3 \epsilon S_1(\epsilon )}{16 \pi ^4 \Lambda L^2}+\frac{m^3 S_0(\epsilon ) S_1(\epsilon )}{64 \pi ^5 L^2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(g^4)\nonumber \end{align}

which recovers the \delta-function Lüscher formula Equation 1 when g=0 and \Lambda\to\infty. At finite \Lambda the surviving 4\epsilon/(\Lambda L) is not an error since it is the cutoff-induced effective range of the contact theory.

If I now substitute2 \Lambda\to\frac{2L}{\pi^2 r_0} I get

-\frac{1}{a_0}+\frac{2 \pi ^2 r_0 \epsilon }{L^2}=\frac{S_0(\epsilon )}{\pi L}+g^2 \left(-\frac{m^3 r_0 \epsilon S_1(\epsilon )}{32 \pi ^2 L^3}+\frac{m^3 S_0(\epsilon ) S_1(\epsilon )}{64 \pi ^5 L^2}+\frac{m^3 S_1(\epsilon )}{8 \pi ^6 L r_0}\right)+\mathcal{O}(g^4)

Recall that \epsilon=\frac{EmL^2}{4\pi^2}=\frac{p^2L^2}{4 \pi^2}. Plugging this into the LHS above shows that the LHS is basically p\cot\delta. So my final expression is

\boxed{ p\cot\delta= \frac{S_0(\epsilon )}{\pi L}+g^2m^3 \left(-\frac{r_0 \epsilon S_1(\epsilon )}{32 \pi ^2 L^3}+\frac{S_0(\epsilon ) S_1(\epsilon )}{64 \pi ^5 L^2}+\frac{S_1(\epsilon )}{8 \pi ^6 L r_0}\right)+\mathcal{O}(g^4) } \tag{3}

  1. Again we have \Lambda dimensionless.

  2. This is the dimensionless analog of \Lambda=\frac{4}{\pi r_0}.

Strategy #1: Finite-cutoff EFT

  • Never take the \Lambda\to\infty limit
  • The correct limit is g\to 0
  • Assume g^4\Lambda\ll 1
  • For fixed g, m, and \mu, use Equation 3 and
    • vary \Lambda and look for regions where it is relatively insensitive to \Lambda.
    • the window in \Lambda where it is insensitive gives a systematic error in this effective theory

I will do this!

Strategy #1a: keep \lambda_{22}=0, but choose the cutoff

With \lambda_{22}=0 I keep the virtue of V_{\rm cut}: the on-shell potential has exactly the OPE logarithmic branch cut, with no \log^2 contamination. The price is that only a(\Lambda) can be tuned, so r_0 becomes a prediction.

But that is precisely what makes the cutoff useful. The contact theory already predicts r_0 = 4/(\pi\Lambda), so I simply choose

\boxed{\ \Lambda = \frac{4}{\pi r_0}\ }

and the predicted effective range lands on the physical one for free. Note \Lambda\approx1.27/r_0, i.e. \Lambda\sim1/r_0 is the natural breakdown scale of a short-range EFT anyway — this is not a trick.

Strategy #1a: what this buys

The three goals align

  • \lambda_{22}=0\ \Longrightarrow on-shell potential keeps exactly the OPE \log discontinuity ✔️
  • \Lambda = 4/(\pi r_0)\ \Longrightarrow both a_0 and r_0 come out right to \mathcal{O}(g^2), with no second counterterm ✔️
  • finite \Lambda\ \Longrightarrow the unabsorbed g^4\Lambda divergence never arises ✔️

This also sharpens the validity condition. Substituting \Lambda = 4/(\pi r_0) into g^2 m\Lambda\lesssim1 gives

\frac{g^2 m}{r_0}\lesssim\frac{\pi}{4}

a statement purely in physical quantities — the cutoff has been eliminated, so there is no plateau to hunt for.

Warning

Small r_0 is dangerous, not safe. The \mathcal{O}(g^2) error coefficient grows like m\Lambda: for m=2, r_0=0.1 (\Lambda=12.7) it reaches -5.25, and by g=0.15 the predicted r has gone negative ⚠️

Why the natural cutoff is inevitable

Equation 3 carries only -1/a_0 on the left because S_0 was defined with the \Lambda\to\infty subtraction. At finite \Lambda the sum retains an \epsilon-dependent residue. Using the large-n shell density 4\pi n^2 dn,

\sum_{\vec n}^{n<\Lambda}\frac{1}{n^2-\epsilon}\approx 4\pi\!\int\! dn\left[1+\frac{\epsilon}{n^2-\epsilon}\right] = 4\pi\Lambda + 4\pi\epsilon\,\mathcal{P}\!\!\int^\Lambda\!\frac{dn}{n^2-\epsilon} = 4\pi\Lambda - \frac{4\pi\epsilon}{\Lambda}

— the same \mathcal{P}\!\int^\Lambda dn/(n^2-\epsilon)\approx-1/\Lambda that produced the -mE/\Lambda in \mathcal{G}_{11}, now seen in the box. Hence

S_0^{(\Lambda)}(\epsilon) = S_0^{(\infty)}(\epsilon) - \frac{4\pi\epsilon}{\Lambda} \, .

Substituting into -\frac{1}{a_0}=\frac{1}{\pi L}S_0^{(\Lambda)}(\epsilon) and moving the residue across:

-\frac{1}{a_0}+\frac{4\epsilon}{L\Lambda} = \frac{1}{\pi L}S_0^{(\infty)}(\epsilon)

Now identify that new term. With \Lambda_{\rm phys}=2\pi\Lambda/L the contact effective range is r_0 = 4/(\pi\Lambda_{\rm phys}) = 2L/(\pi^2\Lambda), and with p^2=mE=4\pi^2\epsilon/L^2,

\frac{r_0}{2}p^2 = \frac{L}{\pi^2\Lambda}\cdot\frac{4\pi^2\epsilon}{L^2} = \frac{4\epsilon}{L\Lambda} \qquad \textit{identical}

The left-hand side promotes itself

\underbrace{-\frac{1}{a_0}+\frac{r_0}{2}p^2}_{\textstyle p\cot\delta\ +\ \mathcal{O}(p^4)} = \frac{1}{\pi L}S_0(\epsilon) i.e. the model-independent Lüscher formula — now derived rather than asserted ✔️

  • This is a real check, not a rearrangement: the r_0=2L/(\pi^2\Lambda) falling out of the finite-volume sum residue is the same r_0=4/(\pi\Lambda_{\rm phys}) obtained independently from the infinite-volume amplitude. Two unrelated routes, same coefficient.
  • At \Lambda = 2L/(\pi^2 r_0) that r_0 is the physical effective range — so choosing the natural cutoff is precisely the statement that the LHS becomes the physical p\cot\delta to \mathcal{O}(p^2), with no second counterterm anywhere.

Consistency checks: does this collapse to the contact theory?

Setting g=0 and r(\Lambda)=0 should return me to the \delta-function results from the beginning of the talk. Both observables do:

Scattering length

The \mathcal{O}(E^0) expression reduces to -\frac{1}{a(\Lambda)}-\frac{2}{\pi}\Lambda = -\frac{1}{a_0} \implies \frac{1}{a(\Lambda)} = \frac{1}{a_0}-\frac{2}{\pi}\Lambda \, , i.e. exactly a(\Lambda)=\dfrac{a_0}{1-\frac{2}{\pi}a_0\Lambda} ✔️

Effective range

The \mathcal{O}(mE) expression reduces to \frac{r_0}{2} = \frac{2}{\pi\Lambda} \implies r_0 = \frac{4}{\pi\Lambda} \, , the cutoff-induced effective range of the contact theory ✔️

  • The second check only works once the -mE/\Lambda term is kept in \mathcal{G}_{11}. Without it the \mathcal{O}(mE) coefficient vanishes identically, and r(\Lambda) would be left to silently absorb the missing 4/(\pi\Lambda) ⚠️

  • Both limits were also checked numerically against a direct evaluation of p\cot\delta = ip - \frac{4\pi}{mT}, agreeing to \sim10^{-8} over a range of g, m, \mu, \Lambda, a(\Lambda) and r(\Lambda).
