Why use QMC?

Curse of Dimensionality

  • Consider a simple 1-d integral using Simpson’s \(k=4\) rule

    \[ \int_a^b dx f(x) = \frac{h}{3}\left(f_0+4f_1+2f_2+\cdots+4f_{N-1}+f_N\right)\quad;\quad h=\frac{b-a}{N},\ f_i=f(x_i) \]

    convergence of this integral \(\sim\mathcal{O}(N^{-k})\)

  • In \(d\) dimensions, convergence scales as \(\sim\mathcal{O}(N^{-k/d})\)

  • Example of deterministic (“grid-based”)

Lifting the curse

Enter Monte Carlo

  • Instead of calculating an observable deterministically, we estimate an observable stochastically

The price we pay is that our estimate has an associated uncertainty

Simplest example of MC estimate

  • Estimate of \(\pi\)

\[ \pi = 4\frac{\mathrm{area\ of\ circle}}{\mathrm{area\ of\ square}}\approx 4 \frac{\mathrm{\#\ red\ points}}{\mathrm{\#\ of\ red\ \&\ blue\ points}} \]

Advantages of (Q)MC

  • Ideal for high-dimensional spaces

  • Valid for both weakly and strongly interacting systems

    • independent of complexity of problem!

  • Error of estimate scales with number of samples \(N_{\mathrm{cfg}}\) as \(\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{cfg}}^{-1/2})\)

    • controllable and systematic
    • independent of dimension \(d\)

But. . .

  • Requires big computers
  • Noisy estimates
  • Can obtain biased results
    • non-ergodic
  • Infamous numerical sign-problem

First steps towards Quantum Monte Carlo

Instead of diagonalizing \(H\), we concentrate on obtaining thermal expectation values of any operator \(O\)

\[ \langle O \rangle = \frac{1}{Z}\mathrm{Tr}\ Oe^{-\beta H} \]

where \(\beta=1/T\) is in inverse temperature and

\[ Z=\mathrm{Tr}\ e^{-\beta H} \]

is the partition function.

The trace is performed over the entire Fock space

Introduce Auxiliary Field \(\phi\)

Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation

\[e^{-\frac{\delta U}{2}\rho_{x,t}^2}\propto\int d\phi_{x,t}e^{-\frac{\phi_{x,t}^2}{2\delta U}+i\phi_{x,t}\rho_{x,t}}\]

“Linearized” the density term

Integration measure \(\mathcal{D}[\phi]\)

\[\int \mathcal{D}[\phi]=\int\Pi_{x,t}d\phi_{x,t}\]

High-dimensional integral!! Enter Monte Carlo. . .

Feynman’s Path Integral

\[ \langle O\rangle = \int\mathcal{D}[\phi]O[\phi]\frac{e^{-S[\phi]}}{Z}= \int\mathcal{D}[\phi]O[\phi]\mathbb{P}[\phi]\]

Importance Sampling

Not all paths are equally important! Want to sample most relevant paths dictated by \(\mathbb{P}[\phi]\).

Anatomy of a QMC simulation

  • Generate ensemble of field configurations \(\phi_i\in\{\phi\}\) stochastically where \[ \mathbb{P}[\phi_i]\sim e^{-S[\phi_i]}/Z \]

  • Estimate \(\langle O\rangle\) \[ \langle O\rangle = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{cfg}}}\sum_{\phi_i\in\{\phi\}}O[\phi_i] + \mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{cfg}}^{-1/2}) \]

    • Perform error analysis
      • Determine autocorrelation
      • bootstrap errors
  • Perform limit extrapolations
    • continuum \(\delta\to 0\)
    • thermodynamic \(N_x\to\infty\)
    • zero-temperature \(\beta\to\infty\)
    • \(m_\pi\to m_\pi^{\mathrm{phys}}\) (LQCD)

The “Sign Problem”

Complex Action \(S[\phi]\)

  • Probability only makes sense when \[ \mathbb{P}[\phi]=\frac{e^{-S[\phi]}}{Z}\in \mathbb{R}_+ \implies S[\phi]\in \mathbb{R} \]

  • \(\exists\) systems when \(S[\phi]\in \mathbb{C}\)

    • non-bipartite lattice
      • cannot divide system into sublattices
    • non-zero chemical potential \(\mu\ne0\)
      • doping (away from half-filling)
      • QCD at finite baryon density

Important

Most interesting problems have a sign problem!

  • Phase transitions at finite density (e.g. hardonic matter to quark/gluon plasma)
  • Doped Mott-Insulators (e.g. high-\(T_c\) superconductivity)
  • Frustrated systems (quantum spin-systems on non-bipartite lattices)
  • Real-time evolution (e.g. non-equilibrium processes)

Reweighting

\[\begin{align} \langle O\rangle &= \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\int \mathcal{D}[\phi]\ O[\phi]\; e^{-S_{\mathcal{R}}[\phi]-iS_{\mathcal{I}}[\phi]}\\ &=\frac{\int \mathcal{D}[\phi]\; O[\phi]e^{-iS_{\mathcal{I}}[\phi]}e^{-S_{\mathcal{R}}[\phi]}}{\int D\phi\; e^{-S_{\mathcal{R}}[\phi]}}\frac{\int D\phi\; e^{-S_{\mathcal{R}}[\phi]}}{\int D\phi\; e^{-S_{\mathcal{R}}[\phi]-iS_{\mathcal{I}}[\phi]}}\\ &=\frac{\langle Oe^{-iS_{\mathcal{I}}}\rangle_{\mathcal{R}}}{\langle e^{-iS_{\mathcal{I}}}\rangle_{\mathcal{R}}} \quad;\quad\mbox{"average phase"}\ =\ \langle e^{-iS_{\mathcal{I}}}\rangle \end{align}\]
